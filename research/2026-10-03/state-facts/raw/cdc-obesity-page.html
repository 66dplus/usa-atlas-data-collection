Adult Obesity Prevalence Maps

For Public Health
Sept. 24, 2026

What to know

The 2025 Adult Obesity Prevalence Maps for 47 states, the District of Columbia (DC), and two U.S. territories show the proportion of adults with obesity. These maps are based on self-reported weight and height from the Behavioral Risk Factor Surveillance System (BRFSS). Maps are also presented by location, age, and race and ethnicity (Updated September 24, 2026). Addressing obesity through early prevention among children and young adults, as well as providing treatment for individuals of all ages, is important for improving overall health and reducing health care costs.

Overview

The CDC 2025 Adult Obesity Prevalence Maps for 47 states, the District of Columbia (DC), Guam, and Puerto Rico show the proportion of adults with a body mass index (BMI) greater than or equal to 30 kg/m2 based on self-reported weight and height. Data are presented by location, age, education level, and race and ethnicity. The data come from the Behavioral Risk Factor Surveillance System, an ongoing state-based, telephone interview survey conducted by CDC and state health departments.

The 2025 maps show that obesity impacts some groups more than others. There are notable differences by individual characteristics, including by location, age, education level, and race and ethnicity.

See adult BMI categories.

Keep in Mind

Preventing and treating obesity is important for health and well-being. These new data highlight the need for obesity prevention and treatment efforts from childhood into adulthood. This includes providing access to and promoting healthy foods, safe places for physical activity, and evidence-based health care services including lifestyle interventions and FDA-approved medications.

Read more about what can be done.

By location across states and territories

In 2025, among U.S. states and territories with available data, all had an obesity prevalence higher than 25%, except the District of Columbia (24.2%). Overall, the Midwest (35.5%) and South (34.8%) had the highest prevalence of obesity, followed by the West (30.7%) and the Northeast (29.6%).

  • One jurisdiction (District of Columbia) had an obesity prevalence between 20% and less than 25%.
  • 7 states had an obesity prevalence between 25% and less than 30%.
  • 19 states had an obesity prevalence between 30% and less than 35%.
  • 21 states, Guam, and Puerto Rico had an obesity prevalence between 35% and less than 40%.
  • No state or territory had an obesity prevalence of 40% or higher.

In 2025, the prevalence of adult obesity varied by age. Middle-aged adults (40–59 years) are about 36% more likely to have obesity than younger adults (18–39 years) and 25% more likely to have obesity than older adults (60 and older). Among U.S. states and territories with available data, the number with an obesity prevalence of 35% or higher is:

  • 6 states and 1 territory among adults 18–39 years.
  • 40 states and 2 territories among adults 40–59 years.
  • 11 states and 1 territory among adults 60 years and older.

In 2025, among U.S. states and territories with available data, obesity prevalence was highest among adults without a high school diploma or equivalent (38.1%) and lowest among college graduates (27.8%).

  • 38.1% of adults without a high school diploma or equivalent had obesity.
  • 35.1% of adults with a high school diploma or equivalent had obesity.
  • 36.8% of adults with some college education had obesity.
  • 27.8% of college graduates had obesity.

Combined BRFSS data from 2023–2025 show notable differences in adult obesity prevalence by race and ethnicity. Among states, territories, or DC with enough data, the number with an obesity prevalence of 35% or higher is:

  • Asian adults: 0 (among 35 states, 1 territory, and DC)
  • White adults: 13 (among 44 states, 2 territories, and DC)
  • Hispanic adults: 32 (among 44 states, 2 territories, and DC)
  • Black adults: 35 (among 44 states and DC)
  • American Indian or Alaska Native adults: 36 (among 44 states)
